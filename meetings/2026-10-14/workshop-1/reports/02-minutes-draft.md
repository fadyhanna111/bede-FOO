## Purpose and framing

Participants set out to review accounting and workflow questions. They said many answers would require internal drafting and later Sharia Board or compliance review. Two question documents were referenced: an accounting set and a workflow set. The precise document versions were not established in the recordings. [A 00:01–02:31; B 63:01–64:58]

## Discussion record

1. **Accounting model and close.** The discussion contrasted gross contractual selling-price receivable with deferred profit and net GL presentation. LMS subledger, Bede and Oracle ERP roles, daily exports, period-close ownership, and no-posting-to-closed-period controls need agreed entries and interfaces. A proposed Odoo option was mentioned without decision. [A 02:32–11:36]
2. **Bank/core reconciliation.** Participants said reconciliation must compare LMS records with independent bank/core cash movement rather than only its own posting log. Transaction- and balance-level feeds, exception ownership and timing were not specified. [A 11:38–14:48]
3. **Pre-disbursement commitments.** Visibility of approved but not yet disbursed financing and possible off-balance treatment was raised. A participant said this is not booked today and could be added; the requested treatment was not finalized. A mismatch between question-document versions was also noted. [A 15:24–18:40]
4. **Non-performing profit.** Two treatments were debated: stop future accrual or move unpaid recognized profit to suspense. Bede-side discussion favored considering suspense, with account-level breakout, recovery handling and a cooling-off period after arrears clear, but the threshold, journals and policy were left for review. [A 18:51–29:32]
5. **Ibra and settlement.** A partial-Ibra screen walkthrough described percentage/manual/unearned-profit options and applying relief to future installments. Bede said it needed to study the behavior. Later questions reopened whether eligible unearned profit includes only not-yet-due installments or unpaid overdue profit, and whether partial settlement shortens the tenor or lowers remaining installments. Early settlement and cheque charge amount/rate/caps remain product-rule questions. [A 30:17–33:51; B 00:04–01:54, 76:18–78:53]
6. **Legal costs, write-off and charity.** Legal costs were described as recoverable expense/receivable offset rather than fee income; example entries need approval. Full write-off was reported as available, partial write-off as requiring development/estimate. Late charges were described as charity rather than income, with posting, payout and approval details open. [B 01:54–07:15, 16:08–19:54]
7. **Top-up and schedule changes.** Bede described a top-up as a new Murabaha contract, including fresh commodity and eligibility, while seeking few manual LMS steps. That differs from a conventional LMS top-up. Participants described fixed contractual profit after sale through rescheduling; days-based deferment, fees, customer acceptance and document signing still need rules. An older deferment scheme that accrued additional profit was mentioned without confirming its applicability here. [B 07:24–15:37, 29:43–42:02]
8. **Delinquency, ECL, notices and collections.** Days-past-due buckets were described as existing, while monetary/count thresholds and stage actions were described as needing detail. Bede's ECL model was said to calculate provisions using LMS-supplied data; the feedback/import contract was not defined. Automated product-configurable customer/internal notifications and channel behavior need requirements. [B 20:23–29:27]
9. **Product/lifecycle boundary.** The group discussed whether the first product is Tawarruq only and whether direct Goods Murabaha is later. Commodity trade, offer and acceptance were described as managed outside the LMS by a mobile/backend flow, with evidence potentially displayed in LMS. Participants agreed in principle that disbursement follows sale confirmation, but the exact sequence among signing, sale, activation and receivable creation was inconsistent in the conversation. A named Sharia approval or final architecture was not produced. [B 42:35–56:55]
10. **Integration operations.** The current backend was said to initiate bank disbursement through KFH/EFTS; prior incidents were mentioned in which bank disbursement or payment succeeded while the LMS was not updated. An LMS-led orchestration alternative was discussed but not selected. The interface needs idempotency, retries, status reconciliation and exception handling. [B 50:06–53:05]
11. **Other scope questions.** Participants verbally set aside Wa’d/promise, Hamish/security deposit, Takaful and multi-currency “for now,” but this was not a signed scope matrix. They discussed one contract per purchase amount, offer validity/credit-bureau reservation, percentage-based pricing and APR disclosure, fees netted from disbursement, and step-up/down or skipped-month repayment templates. Variable installment amounts were described as not currently supported. [B 57:19–75:49]
