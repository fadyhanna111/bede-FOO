## 1. Annex2_LMS_Compliance_FOO_Response.xlsx

This workbook records FOO’s responses to 58 requirements across functionality, regulatory reporting, architecture, performance, customization, security, support, licensing and business continuity. It marks 50 requirements as fully compliant and eight as compliant with provisions. Those provisions concern collections, accounting/CRM/payment/commodity integrations, CBB and AAOIFI reporting, and security certifications. Its comments describe proposed capabilities, dependencies and limitations, making it a useful checklist for follow-up clarification and validation. The compliance labels are FOO’s declarations rather than independently verified results.

Source: [Original workbook](../compliance/Annex2_LMS_Compliance_FOO_Response.xlsx), Technical compliance rows 11–68.

## 2. FOO - Bede - Loan Management System - Technical Response V1.0.pdf

This 61-page proposal explains FOO’s proposed LMS solution, built around Fineract with configuration and extensions for Bede’s financing operations. It covers Murabaha fields and terminology, products, repayment and settlement rules, accounting, delinquency, dashboards, integrations, testing and handover. The proposal includes up to 20 regulatory reports and migration of up to 10,000 active loan agreements, while defining capped effort, client dependencies and exclusions. It estimates four months and relies on requirements sign-offs to establish the detailed build scope. It provides the technical proposal against which Bede’s newer requirements need to be compared.

Source: [Original technical proposal](../proposals/FOO%20-%20Bede%20-%20Loan%20Management%20System%20-%20Technical%20Response%20V1.0.pdf), particularly PDF pages 10–40 and 42–51.

## 3. FOO - Bede - Loan Management System - Commercial Response V1.6.pdf

This 22-page proposal sets out FOO’s pricing and payment terms: USD 270,000 for implementation after discount, a waived five-year subscription license originally priced at USD 134,000, and USD 60,000 annually for maintenance and support over a five-year term. Professional services include 44 man-days of post-live transition support. Payments follow 30% mobilization, 40% UAT sign-off, 20% production sign-off and 10% post-live completion, with taxes excluded. It assumes an estimated four-month delivery and explains how scope changes, extended testing and external delays may affect price and schedule.

Source: [Original commercial proposal](../proposals/FOO%20-%20Bede%20-%20Loan%20Management%20System%20-%20Commercial%20Response%20V1.6.pdf), PDF pages 11 and 14–20.

## 4. Bede LMS Kick-off.pdf

These three-page client meeting minutes record the 1 October 2026 kickoff, including attendees, scope discussions, governance and actions. They state that Bede owns the eligibility engine, while FOO will demonstrate its existing eligibility and scoring solution for consideration. The minutes record monthly steering meetings, weekly updates, commitments accepted in principle, a FOO-hosted development environment and the importance of on-site discovery and hypercare. FOO is to provide a delivery/workshop plan within seven days and a requirement-by-requirement compliance response within 7–15 days; contract terms remain to be formalized. This is a separate client-supplied record from the audio-derived draft minutes.

Source: [Original client minutes](../../meetings/2026-10-01/client-kickoff/Bede%20LMS%20Kick-off.pdf), PDF pages 1–3.

## 5. Bede LMS Project Kickoff Deck.pptx

This 11-slide presentation explains Bede’s reasons for replacing its LMS and outlines the intended scope, integration landscape, delivery phases and governance. It assigns LMS delivery and integrations to FOO, while Bede retains infrastructure, eligibility/origination logic and UAT/production deployments. It presents expectations including 99.9% availability, zero-downtime releases, cutover under two hours, 24×7 support, monthly security releases and Bede ownership of custom intellectual property. The immediate next steps request FOO’s compliance response, architecture, resourcing, workshop calendar and contract inputs. Its SmartArt contains substantial content omitted from the original plain-text extraction and has now been indexed separately.

Source: [Original kickoff deck](../../meetings/2026-10-01/client-kickoff/Bede%20LMS%20Project%20Kickoff%20Deck.pptx), slides 3–10; [SmartArt search supplement](../search-index/Bede%20LMS%20Project%20Kickoff%20Deck-smartart.txt).

## 6. Bede - Foo LMS - Scope and Requirements.pdf

This 21-page document defines Bede’s requested scope for a Murabaha microfinancing LMS, including 39 functional requirements, ten integrations and detailed security, deployment, testing, migration, support and contractual expectations. It covers mobile journeys, the accounting sub-ledger, collections, reporting and data access, alongside on-site discovery, migration reconciliation, 3–6 months of hypercare, a five-year warranty and 24×7 support. It asks FOO to respond against every numbered requirement and provide architecture, delivery, pricing and SLA proposals. Figures in square brackets are indicative and subject to agreement, and several expectations extend beyond the earlier technical and commercial proposals.

Source: [Original requirements document](../requirements/Bede%20-%20Foo%20LMS%20-%20Scope%20and%20Requirements.pdf), PDF pages 2–21.
