Scope and Requirements
1. Purpose
This document sets out the scope, functional and non-functional requirements, delivery model,
support obligations and contractual expectations for the design, build, integration, migration and
ongoing maintenance of a Loan Management System (LMS) supporting Sharia-compliant
(Murabaha-based) microfinancing products offered through the Bede mobile application.
Foo is expected to deliver the LMS and all integrations described herein, including integrations
with the existing mobile application and mobile backend that Foo built.
All figures shown in square brackets are indicative and subject to agreement.

=== PDF PAGE 3 ===
Background, Context, Scope Summary
2. Background and Context
Bede is a fintech licensed by the Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB) under Rulebook Volume 5.
Financing is offered on a Murabaha basis; all products, calculations and accounting must be
Sharia-compliant and subject to Sharia Supervisory Board approval.
Customers apply for and manage financing exclusively through the Bede mobile application.
Staff operate the system through a back-office console (currently CMS).
Infrastructure is hosted in AWS and managed by Bede.
An in-house Eligibility Engine determines customer eligibility; the LMS must consume its
output.
3. Scope Summary
3.1 In scope
1. LMS platform: product configuration, loan lifecycle, repayments, collections, accounting sub-
ledger, reporting data access.
2. All integrations listed in Section 5, including mobile app and mobile backend integration.
3. Back-office console for operations, finance, risk and compliance users.
4. Migration of existing customers and loans, including simulation cycles and cut-over.
5. Deployment packages, documentation, training and knowledge transfer.
6. Hypercare, warranty, 24x7 support and monthly security/hotfix releases.
3.2 Out of scope
Provisioning and management of AWS infrastructure (Bede responsibility; Vendor provides
specifications).
The Eligibility Engine itself (in-house).

4.2 Murabaha mechanics and accounting
FR-01 Fully configurable Sharia-compliant financing products
with no code changes: term, min/max amount, profit rate,
fees, repayment frequency, grace periods, eligibility tags.
FR-02 Product variants driven by: type of good being financed,
customer nationality, credit report outcome, and any
other attribute exposed by the Eligibility Engine.
FR-03 Repayment schedule templates that are customisable and
reusable, including irregular patterns (e.g., pay one
month, skip two), bullet, step-up/step-down and seasonal
schedules.
FR-04 Configurable repayment allocation order (fees / profit /
principal), day-count convention, rounding rules,
weekend and public-holiday handling.
FR-05 Product versioning: changes apply to new contracts only
unless explicitly migrated; full history retained.
FR-06 Sharia Supervisory Board approval recorded against each
product version as an acceptance gate before activation.
RequirementDetails
FR-07 Explicit Murabaha contract model: cost price, profit
amount, total sale price; commodity purchase then sale
to customer.

4.3 Loan lifecycle 
FR-08 Generation of the Murabaha sale contract and
offer/acceptance documents in Arabic and English,
presented and e-signed in the app before disbursement,
stored with the loan record.
FR-09 Profit recognition rules configurable per product; no
compounding of profit; late payment charges routed to a
designated charity account, not to income.
FR-10 Profit rebate (ibra'') rules configurable per product for
early and partial settlement.
FR-11 Full accounting sub-ledger: chart of accounts, automated
journal postings for every financial event, month-end and
year-end close, and audit-ready trial balance.
FR-12 IFRS 9 / AAOIFI staging and provisioning outputs (days
past due, stage classification, expected credit loss
inputs) per loan and portfolio.
FR-13 Daily automated reconciliation of the LMS sub-ledger
against the Core Banking System with exception
reporting.
FR-14 Application: customer applies from the app; the LMS
receives eligibility output and returns eligible products,
terms and indicative schedules.
FR-15 Approval workflow with configurable stages, maker-
checker, and automated approval where rules permit.
FR-16 Disbursement to customer account or
merchant/commodity counterparty, with status tracked
against Core Banking confirmation.
FR-17 Repayment via the app through integrated payment
gateways; repayments received through Core Banking
are also matched and applied.

4.4 Collections and defaults
FR-18 Early settlement with configurable rules per product:
settle without profit, with profit, with a fixed fee, or with a
percentage of the settled amount. Settlement quote
generated in-app.
FR-19 Partial payment with the same configurable charging
options as FR-18 and automatic schedule recalculation.
FR-20 Loan top-up with configurable eligibility, re-underwriting
via the Eligibility Engine, and treatment as new contract
or consolidated contract per product configuration.
FR-21 Multiple concurrent finances per customer, with
portfolio-level exposure limits configurable per product
and customer segment.
FR-22 Deferment for a configurable number of days or months,
with configurable limits per product and treatment of
profit during deferment.
FR-23 Restructuring and rescheduling (staff-initiated) with
maker-checker approval and full audit trail.
FR-24 Write-off, fee waiver, refund and overpayment handling
with maker-checker approval.
FR-25 Customer statements, repayment schedules and
settlement quotes available on demand from the app
and the back-office console.
FR-26 Default and delinquency rules configurable per product
(days past due thresholds, amount thresholds, missed
instalment count).
FR-27 Delinquency staging (e.g., pre-due reminder, 1–30, 31–
60, 61–90, 90+, legal) with configurable stage names,
thresholds and actions.
